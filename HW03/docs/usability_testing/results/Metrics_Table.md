## Counting rule for the Err. / Hes. columns

Written down so the numbers can be audited against the five `session_notes/` files, and so the reader
knows exactly what they are not.

- **Error** = an action the participant took that moved them off the success path and had to be undone
  or redone — wrong destination, blocked submit, wrong record opened. One episode = 1, however long it
  lasted. Failing to *guess* something is not an error until it produces a wrong action.
- **Hesitation** = a point of uncertainty the participant reports in their own words (*"dừng lại một
  chút"*, *"phân vân"*, *"không chắc"*, *"hơi lâu"*). **This is not the ≥3 s pause count §6 asks for** —
  that needs a clock on the recording, which was not run. It is a count of self-declared uncertainty
  points, which is a weaker and different thing, and is labelled as such everywhere it appears.
- **Source is the participant, not the moderator.** Both columns are a **lower bound**. An error the
  participant did not notice, or noticed and did not write down, is invisible here. A moderator-observed
  count would be equal or higher, never lower.

## Task success (aggregate — the "success rate, mean time, errors" table §6 Phase 3 asks for)

Success rate is reported as a **count out of 5**, not a percentage: at n = 5 a percentage claims
precision the sample cannot carry, and `4/5` and `80%` are the same fact with different honesty.

| Task | Complete (of 5) | of which: recovered from a self-caught error | Partial | Fail | Mean time | Errors (self-reported) | Hesitations (self-reported) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 — file the report | **5/5** | 2 (P2 missing image, P4 thin description) | 0/5 | 0/5 | not measured | **4 episodes, 2 of 5 participants** (mean 0.8) | **4, 4 of 5 participants** |
| T2 — find the response | **5/5** | — | 0/5 | 0/5 | not measured | **3 episodes, 3 of 5 participants** (mean 0.6) | **3, 3 of 5 participants** |
| **Both tasks** | 10/10 runs | 2 | 0 | 0 | not measured | **7 episodes, 3 of 5 participants** | **7, 4 of 5 participants** |

Means are given to one decimal and should be read as descriptive only: at n = 5, over a lower-bound
count, "0.8" is a way of writing "4 across five people", not an estimate of a population rate.

**Both tasks completed by all five, so success rate is not where the signal is.** Three of the five
reached the response only after a wrong turn (P2 tried a different menu, P4 opened the wrong request,
P5 tried the bell and fell back to the list), and the SUS spread of 27.5–97.5 says the same thing the
success column cannot: the tasks are *achievable* but the path to them is not *discoverable*. A
success-rate-only reading of this study would conclude the product is fine, which none of the four
open-question answers supports.

**Every one of the seven errors is a navigation or a completeness error; none is a data-loss or a
system error.** Four are "I was in the wrong place" (P2 Events page, P2 wrong menu, P4 several menus,
P4 wrong request), one is the same class on T2 (P5's bell), and two are the form refusing an incomplete
submission — those two are the product working, not failing. Split that way, **5 of 7 errors are
findability and 2 of 7 are successful error prevention**, which is the whole argument of §9 in one line.

**Route taken to the response (T2)** — never steered, so this is a findability result: 4 of 5 used
the requests list, 1 (P3) used the notification bell. P5 tried the bell first and abandoned it.

Nothing was excluded from any aggregate. No mean time is reported because no time was measured.

## Perceived effort — the ordinal measure that stands in for time, and does not replace it

Time on task was not measured. But every participant answered probe 3 (*"Tốc độ / công sức"*), so a
**rank order of perceived effort** is available from the data that does exist. Ranked 1 = least effort,
each rank resting on the participant's own sentence.

| Rank | P | The sentence the rank rests on | Errors | SUS |
| :-: | --- | --- | :-: | :-: |
| 1 | P3 | *"Không có bước nào thừa, việc đính ảnh và gửi diễn ra nhanh"* | 0 | 97.5 |
| 2 | P1 | *"Việc nhập lại khá nhanh, chỉ mất thời gian chọn đúng loại"* | 0 | 80.0 |
| 3 | P5 | *"Không quá chậm, nhưng tôi phải kiểm tra các trường và làm mới trang"* | 1 | 70.0 |
| 4 | P2 | *"Tìm chỗ báo vấn đề và tìm lại yêu cầu hơi lâu"* | 3 | 60.0 |
| 5 | P4 | *"Tốn công nhất là dò nhiều menu và đọc từng yêu cầu để tìm đúng cái mới"* | 3 | 27.5 |

**The three columns agree, in order, for all five participants.** Effort rank, error count and SUS
score move together with no inversion — the two participants who reported no error are the two who
scored 80+, and nobody who hit an error scored above 70. That consistency is worth stating, but it is
**internal consistency, not corroboration**: all three columns are self-report from the same person in
the same sitting, so they can agree while all being wrong together. An observed measure would be needed
to break that dependence, and none was taken.

**What this measure is not.** It is ordinal, not interval — it says P4 found it harder than P2, never
how much or how many seconds. It cannot be averaged, compared against a benchmark, or reported as
"mean time". §6 asks for time on task and this is not that; it is what could be recovered honestly
from the data that was actually collected.

n = 5, **SD = 26.1, range 27.5–97.5**. Reported descriptively — n is too small for a statistical claim.

**The spread is the finding, not the mean.** A mean of 67 would normally read as "acceptable, room to
improve". At SD 26.1 it describes nobody: no participant scored within 6 points of it. The same
product was rated *Best imaginable* by P3 and *Awful* by P4, and the two ratings are consistent with
what each of them wrote — P3 *"hiểu ngay biểu mẫu cần gì"*, P4 *"không chắc nên tìm ở Hồ sơ, Sự kiện
hay Hỗ trợ"*. Experience here is close to binary: it depends on whether the user guesses the right
menu on the first try. Quoting the mean alone would hide that.

## Interpretation note

Five participants surface most of the *findable* problems in a formative, single-user-group study,
but support no statistical claim (no significance, no confidence interval, no benchmark
comparison). Report "3 of 5 participants hit X," give the SUS mean alongside the five individual
scores, and let the severity ranking in the Usability Report carry the argument.
